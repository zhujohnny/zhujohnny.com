# Other Coursework

## Harvard University Extension

I enrolled in and completed 3 courses with the Harvard Extension School:

* **CS50: Introduction to Computer Science**
* **CS51: Abstraction and Design in Computation**
* **CS171: Visualization**

[CS50](https://cs50.harvard.edu/college) taught me the basics of computational thinking and programming in [C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_(programming_language)), and covered topics such as data structures and algorithms, memory management, SQL, HTML, CSS, Javascript, and HTTP requests.
[CS51](https://cs51.io/) in [OCaml](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OCaml), taught me the fundamental concepts of designing computer programs and abstraction. I became more familiar with different paradigms - including imperative, functional, and object-oriented.
Lastly, [CS171](https://www.cs171.org/) taught me tools to visualize large amounts of data using [D3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D3.js). This course played a large part in teaching me how the human visual system processes and perceives images, and how to use software to communicate with users.

In my final project, I worked with another developer to analyze data from the [World Bank logistics performance index](https://lpi.worldbank.org/). The primary questions we wanted to answer were:
* What's a given country's ability to trade?
* With other countries?
* Within region?
* What direction is the country moving?

## Hack Reactor

[Hack Reactor](https://www.hackreactor.com/coding-bootcamp#Curriculum)'s 12-week immersive boot camp in San Francisco paved the way for me to transition full-time to software engineering following my time in business school.

As part of the curriculum, I deepened my understanding of frontend frameworks such as [React](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/React_(JavaScript_library)) and [Angular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angular_(web_framework)), and improved my understanding of backend technologies such as [Node.js](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Node.js), [AWS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Web_Services) and [Docker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Docker_(software)).
Below are two projects I worked on during my time there.

#### Frontend Capstone
Discovery app for local restaurant information and reviews, modeled after Yelp.

The project was an exercise in component design within a larger [microservices architecture](http://microservices.io/).
I personally built the [Restaurant Details](https://github.com/hrsf93-welp/welp-restaurant-details) component, a service provided to a parent restaurant page. The service implements a RESTful API on the backend to retrieve data from a MongoDB database.

#### Backend Capstone
Scaled a crowdfunding application to handle more traffic, modeled after Kickstarter.

I horizontally scaled the application to 10 AWS [EC2](https://aws.amazon.com/ec2/) servers and implemented a [load balancer](https://aws.amazon.com/elasticloadbalancing/), allowing the application to handle 1 million requests in 2 minutes (5x traffic).
I also implemented caching using [Redis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redis), and optimized queries by [denormalizing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denormalization) data and [indexing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Database_index) columns to speed up page load times.
